# Free until 100 users: why now is the time to fix your receipts

_Expense (https://expense.labnotes.org?ref=labnotes.org) is a receipt tracker for freelancers and self-employed filers. It is free until the app reaches 100 users, and the landing page now shows exactly how many spots are left._

I built Expense because every year, the same thing happened: tax season arrived, and my receipts lived in five places — a camera roll, an inbox, a shoebox of paper, a downloads folder, and memory. The apps that promise to fix this want a subscription and access to my Gmail, and they still leave the data entry to me.

Expense takes a different cut at the problem. You get the receipt into the app however the receipt comes to you: take a photo, paste a screenshot, drop a PDF, or forward the email. OCR reads the merchant and the amount, a model suggests the category from IRS Schedule C lines (using your own spending history first), and you press save. One click per receipt, not a form per receipt.

If your mailbox is FastMail, Expense connects to it directly and receipts file themselves as they arrive. Most expense apps only auto-import from Gmail. The FastMail community deserves better, and FastMail's API made it possible without asking anyone to hand over their Gmail password.

Two more things I use every week: mileage, computed from the map at the IRS rate for the drive date, and reconciliation, where you upload a bank statement and the app matches charges against what you already logged, so the deductions you forgot about surface on their own.

There is also an assistant angle. Expense speaks MCP, so you can connect Claude or any MCP client to it and ask "how much did I spend on software this quarter" or "log the drive to the client office on Tuesday" in plain text.

Why free? Because an expense tracker with three users cannot prove anything. Expense is free until the app reaches 100 users. After that a paid plan starts, but the free tier stays (up to 25 invoices a month), so nobody gets locked out of their own data. The landing page now counts the spots in public: as of this post, three of the hundred are claimed.
